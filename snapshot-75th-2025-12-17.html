The sharp end

The six critical jobs

Of 431 jobs, six were flagged critical — the calls involving guns and knives. Four came in the pre-dawn hours. Every one drew a unit on scene in under eight minutes.

Also logged: 15 “serious” jobs — mostly larceny in progress (7) and assaults (8), three of them family assaults.

Methodology

How this was built — and its limits

Source & scope

  • Data: NYPD Calls for Service (Year-to-Date), NYC Open Data dataset n2zq-pubd — the police department's own computer-aided dispatch (CAD) log.
  • Slice: all 431 jobs where precinct = 75 and incident date = December 17, 2025. All 431 carried valid coordinates.
  • Why this day: the most recent full month available is December 2025. December 17 is a midweek day clear of the holiday week, with a job count (431) right at the precinct's December daily average — chosen to be ordinary, not anomalous.
  • Why the 75th: the busiest residential precinct in the city, with the fullest mix of call types. The 14th (Midtown South) and 40th (Mott Haven) logged marginally more jobs in December but skew commercial.

Definitions & caveats

  • “Response time” measures job-created to first-unit-arrived. It excludes the time a 911 caller spends on the line before a call-taker creates the job, so true door-to-door response is somewhat longer.
  • The 40% with no arrival time are largely officer-initiated runs — visibility patrol, transit inspections, ShotSpotter activations — that self-clear without a separate arrival stamp. They are not failures to respond. Timing statistics use only the 260 jobs with an arrival time.
  • Medians, not means. A handful of low-priority jobs sat in queue for hours and badly skew the averages; medians are the honest central measure.
  • Jobs, not incidents or arrests. One incident can generate several jobs, and this dataset carries no arrest or disposition outcome.
  • Priority uses the NYPD “CIP” field (Critical / Serious / Non-Critical / Non-CIP). “Families” are this project's grouping of 94 raw call descriptions, listed in full in the dataset.

Made with AI assistance

This infographic was assembled with an AI assistant. What was and wasn't checked:

  • Verified: every figure is computed directly from the raw NYC Open Data records — totals, hourly counts, priority splits and response-time medians were all derived in code from the 431 downloaded rows, not estimated.
  • Verified: all 431 jobs were geocoded in the source data; map points are the city's own coordinates.
  • Not independently verified: the accuracy of the underlying police records themselves, including how each call was classified and timestamped by the NYPD.
  • Judgment calls: the grouping of 94 call descriptions into families is editorial. The single-day snapshot is illustrative and should not be read as the precinct's typical pattern.
